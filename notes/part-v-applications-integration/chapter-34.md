## 5.2 — Volumes of Revolution (Disks and Washers)

Rotating a plane region about an axis produces a **solid of revolution**. The definite integral lets us compute the exact volume of such solids by summing the volumes of thin cross-sectional slices. The two most common slicing methods are the **disk method** (used when the solid has no hole) and the **washer method** (used when the solid has a hollow core).

### Key Formulas

**Disk Method (rotation about the x-axis)**

If $y = f(x) \ge 0$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, rotating the region under $f$ about the $x$-axis produces a solid whose volume is:

$$V = \pi \int_a^b [f(x)]^2\,dx$$

Each cross-section perpendicular to the $x$-axis is a **disk** of radius $f(x)$.

**Washer Method (rotation about the x-axis)**

If $R(x) \ge r(x) \ge 0$ on $[a, b]$, rotating the region between $y = R(x)$ and $y = r(x)$ about the $x$-axis produces a solid with a hole. The volume is:

$$V = \pi \int_a^b \Big([R(x)]^2 - [r(x)]^2\Big)\,dx$$

Each cross-section is a **washer** with outer radius $R(x)$ and inner radius $r(x)$.

### Worked Example

**Example 5.2.1**

A sphere of radius $R$ can be generated by rotating the semicircle $y = \sqrt{R^2 - x^2}$ about the $x$-axis. Use the disk method to derive the familiar volume formula $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$.

**Step 1:** Identify the bounds. The semicircle extends from $x = -R$ to $x = R$.

**Step 2:** Apply the disk method.

$$V = \pi \int_{-R}^{R} \left(\sqrt{R^2 - x^2}\right)^2 dx = \pi \int_{-R}^{R} (R^2 - x^2)\,dx$$

**Step 3:** Evaluate the integral.

$$V = \pi \left[R^2 x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-R}^{R}$$
$$= \pi \left[\left(R^3 - \frac{R^3}{3}\right) - \left(-R^3 + \frac{R^3}{3}\right)\right]$$
$$= \pi \left[\frac{2R^3}{3} + \frac{2R^3}{3}\right] = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$$

**Answer:** The volume of a sphere is $\boxed{\dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3}$.

### Worked Example

**Example 5.2.2**

Find the volume obtained by rotating the region bounded by $y = \sqrt{x}$ and $y = 0$ about the line $y = 2$.

**Step 1:** Identify the bounds in $x$. The curves intersect at $x = 0$ and $x = 4$ (since $\sqrt{4} = 2$ is the intersection with $y=2$ if we consider the region, but actually the region under $y=\sqrt{x}$ from $x=0$ to $x=4$ is rotated). Let's be precise: the region is bounded by $y = \sqrt{x}$, $x = 0$, $x = 4$, and $y = 0$.

**Step 2:** Determine outer and inner radii.

When rotating about $y = 2$, the outer radius is $R(x) = 2 - 0 = 2$ and the inner radius 
is $r(x) = 2 - \sqrt{x}$.

**Step 3:** Set up the washer integral.

$$V = \pi \int_0^4 \Big[2^2 - (2-\sqrt{x})^2\Big]\,dx$$

**Step 4:** Expand and evaluate.

$$V = \pi \int_0^4 \left[4 - (4 - 4\sqrt{x} + x)\right]dx = \pi \int_0^4 (4\sqrt{x} - x)\,dx$$
$$= \pi \left[\frac{8}{3}x^{3/2} - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4$$
$$= \pi \left[\frac{8}{3}(8) - \frac{16}{2}\right] = \pi \left(\frac{64}{3} - 8\right) = \pi \cdot \frac{40}{3}$$

**Answer:** The volume is $\boxed{\dfrac{40\pi}{3}\text{ cubic units}}$.

### Practice Problems

- Find the volume of the solid obtained by rotating $y = x^2$ on $[0, 2]$ about the $x$-axis.
- Find the volume of the solid obtained by rotating the region between $y = x$ and $y = x^2$ about the $x$-axis.
- Find the volume of the solid obtained by rotating $y = \sqrt{x}$ on $[0, 4]$ about the $y$-axis. (Use the disk method with respect to $y$.)

### Study Tips

- Always draw the axis of rotation and label the outer radius $R$ and inner radius $r$.
- When rotating about a line $y = k$ or $x = k$, the radius is the *distance* from the curve to the axis: $R = |\text{curve} - k|$.
- Washers subtract the inner area from the outer area; never forget the square: $\pi(R^2 - r^2)$, not $\pi(R-r)^2$.
- If the axis of rotation is a coordinate axis, the formulas simplify; otherwise account for the shift.

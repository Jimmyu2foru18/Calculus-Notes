3.8 — Implicit Differentiation

Most curves in calculus — circles, ellipses, hyperbolas — are not functions $y = f(x)$ at all. They are defined implicitly by an equation involving both $x$ and $y$, like $x^2 + y^2 = 1$. Implicit differentiation lets us find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ without solving for $y$ explicitly, by differentiating both sides of the equation and remembering that $y$ is a function of $x$.

Key Technique

Procedure for Implicit Differentiation

  1. Differentiate both sides of the equation with respect to $x$.
  2. Treat $y$ as a function of $x$: when differentiating $y$, write $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ (or $y'$).
  3. Collect all terms containing $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ on one side.
  4. Factor out $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ and solve for it.

Worked Example — Circle

Example 3.8.1

Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$.

Step 1: Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$.

$$\frac{d}{dx}(x^2) + \frac{d}{dx}(y^2) = \frac{d}{dx}(25)$$ $$2x + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$$

Step 2: Solve for $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.

$$2y\frac{dy}{dx} = -2x$$ $$\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x}{y}$$
Show Answer

Answer: For any point $(x,y)$ on the circle, the slope is $\boxed{-\dfrac{x}{y}}$.

Practice Problems

  1. Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for the ellipse $4x^2 + 9y^2 = 36$.
  2. Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for $x^3 + y^3 = 6xy$ (the folium of Descartes).
  3. Find the equation of the tangent line to $x^2 + xy + y^2 = 7$ at the point $(1, 2)$.

Study Tips

  • When differentiating $y^n$, use the Chain Rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}(y^n) = n y^{n-1} \dfrac{dy}{dx}$.
  • Don't solve for $y$ first — that's usually harder and often impossible in closed form.
  • After finding $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, check its sign against the curve's shape to verify.
  • At points where the tangent is vertical, $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ is undefined but $\dfrac{dx}{dy}$ may be zero.

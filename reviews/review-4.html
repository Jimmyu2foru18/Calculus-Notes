Review: Differentiation Rules

This sheet covers advanced trigonometric derivatives, related rates setup, the chain rule, implicit differentiation, and logarithmic differentiation — key tools for handling composite and implicitly-defined functions.

Derivatives of Inverse Trig Functions

These derivatives require implicit differentiation to derive. Memorize the formulas and their domain restrictions.

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[\sin^{-1}(x)\right] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}, \quad |x| < 1 \]
Derivative of arcsine
\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[\cos^{-1}(x)\right] = \frac{-1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}, \quad |x| < 1 \]
Derivative of arccosine
\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[\tan^{-1}(x)\right] = \frac{1}{1+x^2} \]
Derivative of arctangent

Watch the domains: $\sin^{-1}$ and $\cos^{-1}$ require $|x| < 1$. $\tan^{-1}$ is defined for all real $x$.

Related Rates Setup

Related rates problems involve finding how one quantity changes with respect to time when other related quantities are also changing.

  1. Draw a diagram and label all relevant quantities.
  2. Write an equation relating the quantities (geometry, physics, etc.).
  3. Differentiate both sides with respect to $t$.
  4. Substitute known values and solve for the unknown rate.

Key principle: All variables are functions of time $t$. Differentiate first, then substitute known values.

Related Rates Example: Shadow Problem

A 6 ft tall person walks away from a 15 ft lamppost at 5 ft/s. How fast is the tip of the shadow moving when the person is 10 ft from the pole?

\[ \frac{x}{6} = \frac{x+y}{15} \implies \frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{6}{9} \cdot \frac{d(x+y)}{dt} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 5 = \frac{10}{3} \text{ ft/s} \]
Similar triangles rate setup

The Chain Rule

If $y = f(u)$ and $u = g(x)$, then $y$ is a composite function of $x$:

\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \]
Chain rule in Leibniz notation
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) \]
Chain rule in prime notation

Generalized power rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}[u^n] = nu^{n-1} \cdot u'$, where $u$ is any differentiable function of $x$.

Applying the Chain Rule

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[\sin(x^2)] = \cos(x^2) \cdot 2x \]
Chain rule example: outer $\sin$, inner $x^2$
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[(3x+1)^5] = 5(3x+1)^4 \cdot 3 = 15(3x+1)^4 \]
Generalized power rule example

Common mistake: Forgetting to multiply by $u'$. $\dfrac{d}{dx}[\sin(x^2)] = \cos(x^2)$, not $\cos(x^2) \cdot 2x$? No — you MUST include $u'$.

Chain Rule with Multiple Layers

For compositions of three or more functions, apply the chain rule repeatedly.

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[\sin(e^{x^2})] = \cos(e^{x^2}) \cdot e^{x^2} \cdot 2x \]
Triple composition example

Implicit Differentiation

When $y$ is not isolated, differentiate both sides of the equation with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a function of $x$ and using the chain rule on every $y$ term.

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[y^3] = 3y^2 \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} \]
Differentiating an implicit $y$ term

Goal: Solve for $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$. After differentiating, collect all $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ terms on one side, factor, and solve.

Worked Example: Circle

Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for $x^2 + y^2 = 25$:

\[ 2x + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x}{y} \]
Implicit differentiation of $x^2 + y^2 = 25$

Logarithmic Differentiation

For complicated products, quotients, or powers, take $\ln$ of both sides first, then differentiate implicitly:

\[ y = x^x \implies \ln y = x \ln x \implies \frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} = \ln x + 1 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = x^x(\ln x + 1) \]
Logarithmic differentiation of $y = x^x$

When to use: Use logarithmic differentiation when the function is a product of many factors, a variable raised to a variable power, or has a complicated structure.

Practice Problems

  1. Find $\dfrac{d}{dx}[\sec^{-1}(5x^3)]$ using the inverse trig rule and chain rule.
  2. Find the 4th derivative of $f(x) = 3x^6 + 5x^5 - 3x^4 + 3x^3 - 9x^2 + 10x - 4$.
  3. Find the 4th derivative of $f(x) = \cos(2x)$.
  4. Differentiate $f(x) = x^{5/3} + 2x^{-2} - 5x^{-2}$ using the power rule.
  5. Differentiate $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x^5}$ by rewriting as $x^{5/3}$.
  6. Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ at the point $(3, 4)$.
  7. Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for $x^3 + y^3 = 6xy$ (folium of Descartes).
  8. Use logarithmic differentiation to find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for $y = x^x$.
  9. Use logarithmic differentiation for $y = \dfrac{(x+1)^3(x-2)^2}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}$.
  10. Airplane problem: An airplane flies at constant height 6 km toward a radar station. If the distance $s$ is decreasing at 400 km/hr when $s = 10$ km, find the horizontal speed.
  11. Shadow problem: A 6 ft person walks away from a 15 ft lamppost at 5 ft/s. Find the rate the tip of the shadow moves when the person is 10 ft from the base.

Review: Applications II

This sheet covers optimization, linear approximation and differentials, the Mean Value Theorem and Rolle's Theorem, and L'Hôpital's Rule for evaluating indeterminate limits.

Optimization Method

Optimization problems ask for the maximum or minimum value of a quantity subject to constraints.

  1. Read the problem carefully and identify the quantity to optimize.
  2. Draw a diagram and label variables.
  3. Write the objective function $f(x)$ to maximize or minimize.
  4. Use the constraint equation to eliminate extra variables.
  5. Find critical numbers of the reduced objective function.
  6. Evaluate at critical numbers and endpoints (if closed interval).
  7. Answer the original question with appropriate units.

Key principle: Always reduce to a single-variable function before differentiating. Use the constraint to express everything in terms of one variable.

Worked Example: Box with Maximum Volume

A box with a square base and open top is made from 48 in² of cardboard. Find dimensions for maximum volume.

\[ \text{Surface area: } x^2 + 4xh = 48 \implies h = \frac{48 - x^2}{4x} \] \[ V(x) = x^2h = \frac{48x - x^3}{4} \] \[ V'(x) = \frac{48 - 3x^2}{4} = 0 \implies x = 4, h = 2 \]
Max volume: $4 \times 4 \times 2$ inches

Linear Approximation (Linearization)

Near $x = a$, a differentiable function $f$ can be approximated by its tangent line:

\[ L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a) \]
Linear approximation (linearization) of $f$ at $a$

Intuition: The tangent line at $(a, f(a))$ is the best linear approximation to $f$ near $a$. As $x \to a$, the error in approximation goes to zero faster than $|x - a|$.

Differentials

The differential $dy$ approximates the actual change $\Delta y$ for a small change $dx$:

\[ dy = f'(x)\, dx \]
Differential of $y = f(x)$
\[ \Delta y \approx dy = f'(x)\, dx \]
Approximation: actual change $\approx$ differential

Important: $dy$ approximates $\Delta y$ only for small $dx$. The approximation error grows as $dx$ gets larger.

Worked Example: Approximation

Use linearization to approximate $\sqrt{4.1}$:

\[ f(x) = \sqrt{x}, \quad a = 4, \quad f'(x) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} \] \[ L(x) = 2 + \frac{1}{4}(x - 4) = 2 + \frac{x - 4}{4} \] \[ L(4.1) = 2 + \frac{0.1}{4} = 2.025 \quad (\text{actual } \approx 2.0248) \]
Linear approximation of $\sqrt{4.1}$

Rolle's Theorem

If $f$ satisfies three conditions on $[a, b]$, then there is at least one point where $f'(c) = 0$:

\[ \text{If } \begin{cases} f \text{ continuous on } [a, b] \\ f \text{ differentiable on } (a, b) \\ f(a) = f(b) \end{cases} \implies \exists c \in (a, b) : f'(c) = 0 \]
Rolle's Theorem

Geometric meaning: There is at least one horizontal tangent between two points at the same height.

Mean Value Theorem (MVT)

The MVT generalizes Rolle's Theorem to cases where $f(a) \neq f(b)$:

\[ \text{If } \begin{cases} f \text{ continuous on } [a, b] \\ f \text{ differentiable on } (a, b) \end{cases} \implies \exists c \in (a, b) : f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a} \]
Mean Value Theorem

Interpretation: The instantaneous rate of change equals the average rate of change at some point $c$ in the interval.

Consequences of the MVT

  • If $f'(x) = 0$ on $(a, b)$ and $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, then $f$ is constant on $[a, b]$.
  • If $f'(x) > 0$ on $(a, b)$, then $f$ is strictly increasing on $[a, b]$.
  • If $f'(x) < 0$ on $(a, b)$, then $f$ is strictly decreasing on $[a, b]$.
  • If $f'(x) = g'(x)$ on $(a, b)$, then $f(x) = g(x) + C$ on $[a, b]$.

Continuity at endpoints is required. A function with a jump discontinuity (e.g., step function) can violate the MVT even if differentiable on $(a, b)$.

L'Hôpital's Rule

For indeterminate forms, differentiate the numerator and denominator separately:

\[ \text{If } \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \text{ is } \frac{0}{0} \text{ or } \frac{\infty}{\infty}, \text{ then } \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)} \]
L'Hôpital's Rule (indeterminate forms)

Indeterminate forms covered: $\frac{0}{0}$, $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$, $0 \cdot \infty$, $\infty - \infty$, $0^0$, $1^\infty$, $\infty^0$ (convert to $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$ first).

Applying L'Hôpital's Rule

Check conditions every time. If the new limit is still indeterminate, apply the rule again.

\[ \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} \xrightarrow{\text{L'H}} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cos x}{1} = 1 \]
Classic $0/0$ example
\[ \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\ln x}{x} \xrightarrow{\text{L'H}} \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1/x}{1} = 0 \]
Classic $\infty/\infty$ example

Converting to Indeterminate Form

Some limits are not initially in $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$ form:

\[ \lim_{x \to 0^+} x \ln x = \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{\ln x}{1/x} = \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{1/x}{-1/x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0^+} (-x) = 0 \]
$0 \cdot \infty$ converted to $\infty/\infty$

Do NOT apply L'Hôpital's Rule unless the form is indeterminate. $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x^2}{x} = 0$ is NOT $0/0$ after the first simplification — cancel first.

Practice Problems

  1. Maximize the area of a rectangle with perimeter 40 cm.
  2. Find the dimensions of a cylinder with volume $1000\pi$ cm³ that minimizes surface area.
  3. Use linearization to approximate $\sqrt[3]{8.1}$ near $a = 8$.
  4. Find the differential $dy$ for $y = x^3 + 2x$ when $x = 1$ and $dx = 0.1$.
  5. Verify that $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$ satisfies the MVT on $[0, 2]$ and find all such $c$.
  6. Verify that $f(x) = \ln x$ satisfies the MVT on $[1, e]$ and find $c$.
  7. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{e^x - 1}{x}$ using L'Hôpital's Rule.
  8. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\sin(3x)}{\tan(5x)}$ using L'Hôpital's Rule.
  9. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to \infty} \dfrac{\ln x}{x^2}$ using L'Hôpital's Rule.
  10. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0^+} x^2 \ln x$ by converting to an indeterminate fraction.
  11. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to \infty} (1 + \frac{1}{x})^x$ by converting to exponential/log form.

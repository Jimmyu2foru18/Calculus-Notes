Review: Derivative Basics

This sheet covers the definition of the derivative, differentiability vs. continuity, basic differentiation rules (constant, power, sum/difference), product and quotient rules, and derivatives of the six trig functions.

Definition of the Derivative

The derivative of $f$ at $x$ is the slope of the tangent line, defined as the limit of the difference quotient:

\[ f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} \]
Limit definition of the derivative
\[ f'(a) = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a} \]
Alternative form at a specific point

Geometric meaning: $f'(a)$ is the slope of the tangent line to $y = f(x)$ at the point $(a, f(a))$. It also gives the instantaneous rate of change.

Differentiability Implies Continuity

If $f$ is differentiable at $x = a$, then $f$ is continuous at $x = a$. The converse is false.

\[ f'(a) \text{ exists } \implies \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a) \]
Differentiability implies continuity

Three requirements for differentiability: (1) $f$ is continuous at $x = c$, (2) the graph is smooth (no sharp corners), (3) no vertical tangent at $x = c$.

Constant and Power Rules

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[c] = 0 \]
Constant rule
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[x^n] = nx^{n-1} \quad \text{(Power Rule, for any real } n\text{)} \]
Power rule
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[x^{1/2}] = \frac{1}{2}x^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} \]
Example: $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$

Sum, Difference, and Constant Multiple Rules

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[f(x) \pm g(x)] = f'(x) \pm g'(x) \]
Sum / difference rule
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[c \cdot f(x)] = c \cdot f'(x) \]
Constant multiple rule

Worked example: $f(x) = 3x^4 + 5x^2 + 10 \implies f'(x) = 12x^3 + 10x$.

Negative and Fractional Exponents

Rewrite using exponent rules before differentiating:

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[x^{-n}\right] = -nx^{-n-1}, \quad \frac{d}{dx}\left[x^{m/n}\right] = \frac{m}{n}x^{\frac{m}{n}-1} \]
Negative and fractional power rules

Pitfall: $\dfrac{d}{dx}[x^{-n}] \neq nx^{-n-1}$ — the sign matters: it is $-nx^{-n-1}$.

Product Rule

If $f(x) = u(x) \cdot v(x)$, then:

\[ f'(x) = u(x)v'(x) + v(x)u'(x) \]
Product rule

Common mistake: The derivative of a product is not the product of the derivatives. $[uv]' \neq u'v'$.

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[x^2 \sin x] = x^2 \cos x + 2x \sin x \]
Product rule example

Quotient Rule

If $f(x) = \dfrac{u(x)}{v(x)}$ with $v(x) \neq 0$, then:

\[ f'(x) = \frac{v(x)u'(x) - u(x)v'(x)}{[v(x)]^2} \]
Quotient rule

Mnemonic: "Lo D Hi minus Hi D Lo, over Lo Lo." The order matters — the minus sign is between the two terms in the numerator.

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{x^2}{\cos x}\right] = \frac{\cos x \cdot 2x - x^2 \cdot (-\sin x)}{\cos^2 x} = \frac{2x\cos x + x^2\sin x}{\cos^2 x} \]
Quotient rule example

When to Use Each Rule

SituationUse
Function is a product of two (or more) simpler functionsProduct rule
Function is a quotient of two functionsQuotient rule
Quotient with a constant numeratorRewrite as constant $\cdot$ (denominator)$^{-1}$; use chain rule
Product of 3+ functionsProduct rule iteratively, or logarithmic differentiation

Derivatives of Sine and Cosine

These require a geometric proof using the squeeze theorem:

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[\sin x] = \cos x \]
Derivative of sine
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[\cos x] = -\sin x \]
Derivative of cosine

Memory tip: "Sine keeps its sign, cosine changes." The derivative of $\sin x$ is $\cos x$ (same sign), and the derivative of $\cos x$ is $-\sin x$ (sign change).

Derivatives of Tangent and Secant

Derived from the quotient rule applied to $\sin x / \cos x$:

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[\tan x] = \sec^2 x \]
Derivative of tangent
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[\sec x] = \sec x \tan x \]
Derivative of secant

Derivatives of Cotangent and Cosecant

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[\cot x] = -\csc^2 x \]
Derivative of cotangent
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[\csc x] = -\csc x \cot x \]
Derivative of cosecant
FunctionDerivative
$\sin x$$\cos x$
$\cos x$$-\sin x$
$\tan x$$\sec^2 x$
$\cot x$$-\csc^2 x$
$\sec x$$\sec x \tan x$
$\csc x$$-\csc x \cot x$

Watch the negative signs: The derivatives of $\cos x$, $\cot x$, and $\csc x$ all carry a negative sign. The derivatives of $\sin x$, $\sec x$, and $\tan x$ do not.

Practice Problems

  1. Use the limit definition to find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = \sqrt{3x - 4}$.
  2. Use the limit definition to find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = \dfrac{5}{4x + 1}$.
  3. Differentiate $f(x) = x^{1/2} - 5x^2$ using the power rule.
  4. Differentiate $f(x) = 3x + 7x^{-3}$.
  5. Differentiate $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x^5} + \dfrac{5}{x^2}$.
  6. Differentiate $f(x) = 3x + 4x^{-1} - 8x^{-3}$.
  7. Find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = x^2 \sin x$ using the product rule.
  8. Find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{\cos x}$ using the quotient rule.
  9. Find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = \tan x \cdot \sec x$ using the product rule.
  10. Find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{x^2}$ using the quotient rule.

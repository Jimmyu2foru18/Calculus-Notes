Review: Limits Foundations

This sheet covers the formal definition of a limit, epsilon-delta proofs, limit laws, the squeeze theorem, and continuity. Master these before moving to advanced limits and derivatives.

Definition of a Limit

We write $\lim\limits_{x \to a} f(x) = L$ when the values of $f(x)$ can be made arbitrarily close to $L$ by taking $x$ sufficiently close to $a$ (but $x \neq a$).

\[ \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L \iff \forall \epsilon > 0, \exists \delta > 0 : 0 < |x - a| < \delta \implies |f(x) - L| < \epsilon \]
Precise epsilon-delta definition

Key insight: The limit describes the value a function approaches, not necessarily the value at the point. $f(a)$ may be undefined, different from $L$, or $L$ may not exist at all.

Left and Right Limits

A two-sided limit exists if and only if both one-sided limits exist and are equal.

NotationMeaning
$\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x)$Left-hand limit ($x$ approaches $a$ from below)
$\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x)$Right-hand limit ($x$ approaches $a$ from above)
$\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$Two-sided limit exists iff left = right

Vertical Asymptotes

The line $x = a$ is a vertical asymptote of $y = f(x)$ if at least one of the following holds:

  • $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \infty$
  • $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = -\infty$
  • $\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = \infty$ or $\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = -\infty$

Common mistake: A function can have a vertical asymptote even if it is defined at that point. Check the limit behavior, not just $f(a)$.

The Epsilon-Delta Game

To prove $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$, we must show that for every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists a $\delta > 0$ such that $x$ within $\delta$ of $a$ guarantees $f(x)$ within $\epsilon$ of $L$.

\[ 0 < |x - a| < \delta \implies |f(x) - L| < \epsilon \]
The epsilon-delta implication chain

Strategy: Start with $|f(x) - L| < \epsilon$ and algebraically derive a bound of the form $|x - a| < \delta$. Use that bound as your $\delta$.

Proof Template: Linear Functions

For $f(x) = mx + b$, proving $\lim_{x \to a} (mx + b) = ma + b$:

\[ |(mx + b) - (ma + b)| = |m||x - a| < \epsilon \implies |x - a| < \frac{\epsilon}{|m|} \]
Choose $\delta = \epsilon / |m|$

Proof Template: Quadratic Functions

For $f(x) = x^2$, proving $\lim_{x \to a} x^2 = a^2$:

\[ |x^2 - a^2| = |x - a||x + a| < \delta \cdot (|a| + \delta + |a|) \]
Restrict $\delta \le 1$, then $|x + a| \le 2|a| + 1$

Pitfall: You cannot simply set $\delta = \epsilon / |x + a|$ because $\delta$ must depend only on $a$ and $\epsilon$, not on $x$.

Limit Laws

Assume $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$ and $\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = M$ exist. Then:

LawFormula
Sum$\lim_{x \to a} [f(x) + g(x)] = L + M$
Difference$\lim_{x \to a} [f(x) - g(x)] = L - M$
Constant Multiple$\lim_{x \to a} [c \cdot f(x)] = cL$
Product$\lim_{x \to a} [f(x) \cdot g(x)] = LM$
Quotient$\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{L}{M}$, provided $M \neq 0$
Power$\lim_{x \to a} [f(x)]^n = L^n$
Root$\lim_{x \to a} \sqrt[n]{f(x)} = \sqrt[n]{L}$ (for $n$ even, require $L > 0$)

The Squeeze (Sandwich) Theorem

If $g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x)$ near $a$ (except possibly at $a$) and $\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = \lim_{x \to a} h(x) = L$, then $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$.

\[ \text{If } g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x) \text{ and } \lim_{x \to a} g(x) = \lim_{x \to a} h(x) = L, \text{ then } \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L \]
Squeeze Theorem

Classic example: $\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin(1/x) = 0$ because $-x^2 \le x^2\sin(1/x) \le x^2$ and both outer limits are $0$.

Evaluating Limits Algebraically

Always try direct substitution first. If you get $0/0$, use algebraic manipulation.

Common mistake: Do not conclude a limit is $\infty$ just because the denominator approaches $0$. You must check the left and right limits separately.

\[ \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{0}{0} \text{ — factor and cancel, or rationalize, or use LCD} \]
Indeterminate form $0/0$

Definition of Continuity

A function $f$ is continuous at $x = a$ if three conditions hold:

  1. $f(a)$ is defined
  2. $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ exists
  3. $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$

Important theorem: If $f$ is differentiable at $a$, then $f$ is continuous at $a$. However, continuity does NOT imply differentiability (e.g., $f(x) = |x|$ at $x = 0$).

Continuity Theorems

If $f$ and $g$ are continuous at $a$:

  • Sum/Difference: $f \pm g$ is continuous at $a$
  • Product: $f \cdot g$ is continuous at $a$
  • Quotient: $f/g$ is continuous at $a$ if $g(a) \neq 0$
  • Composition: $f \circ g$ is continuous at $a$ if $g$ continuous at $a$ and $f$ continuous at $g(a)$
  • Inverse: If $f$ is continuous and one-to-one on $[a, b]$, then $f^{-1}$ is continuous on $[f(a), f(b)]$
\[ \text{Polynomials, rationals, root, trig, inverse trig, exponential, and log functions are continuous on their domains.} \]
Continuous function families

Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT): If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and $N$ is between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$, then there exists $c \in [a, b]$ with $f(c) = N$.

Practice Problems

  1. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 1} \dfrac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}$ by factoring.
  2. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 3} \dfrac{4x - 12}{x^2 - x - 6}$ by factoring.
  3. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 4} \dfrac{x - 4}{\sqrt{x} - 2}$ by rationalizing the denominator.
  4. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\frac{1}{x-4} + \frac{1}{4}}{x}$ by finding the LCD.
  5. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 1} \left(\frac{1}{x-1} - \frac{2}{x^2-1}\right)$ by combining into a single fraction.
  6. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 5} \dfrac{\sqrt{x+4} - 2}{3 - \sqrt{x-1}}$ by rationalizing the numerator.
  7. Find the domain and range of $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 + 3x + 2}$ and identify any holes or asymptotes.
  8. Use the Squeeze Theorem to show $\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin(1/x) = 0$.
  9. Prove using epsilon-delta that $\lim_{x \to 3} (2x + 1) = 7$.
  10. Determine where $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 + 2x - 3}{x^2 - 9}$ is discontinuous and classify each discontinuity.

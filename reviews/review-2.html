Review: Advanced Limits

This sheet covers one-sided limits, infinite limits with vertical asymptotes, limits at infinity with horizontal and oblique asymptotes, continuity on intervals, and the Intermediate Value Theorem.

Left and Right Limits

One-sided limits examine the behavior of $f(x)$ as $x$ approaches $a$ from only one side.

\[ \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = L \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = M \]
Left-hand and right-hand limit notation
ConditionConclusion
$\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = L$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$
$\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) \neq \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x)$Two-sided limit does not exist
Either one-sided limit is $\pm\infty$Two-sided limit is $\pm\infty$ or DNE

Evaluating One-Sided Limits

When a piecewise function changes definition at $x = a$, the two-sided limit may not exist even though both one-sided limits exist.

\[ f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2 - 1, & x < 2 \\ 3x - 4, & x \ge 2 \end{cases} \implies \lim_{x \to 2^-} f(x) = 3, \quad \lim_{x \to 2^+} f(x) = 2 \]
Piecewise function example — limit DNE

Common mistake: For piecewise functions, always substitute the adjacent expression for each one-sided limit, not the value at the point.

Limits of Piecewise and Step Functions

For greatest integer (floor) functions and step functions, use the graph or definition to determine one-sided behavior.

\[ \lim_{x \to n^-} \lfloor x \rfloor = n - 1, \quad \lim_{x \to n^+} \lfloor x \rfloor = n \]
Floor function one-sided limits at integer $n$

Infinite Limits

We write $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \infty$ when $f(x)$ grows without bound as $x \to a$. Similarly for $-\infty$. These are not real numbers — the limit does not exist in the strict sense.

\[ \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \infty \iff \forall M > 0, \exists \delta > 0 : 0 < |x - a| < \delta \implies f(x) > M \]
Precise definition of an infinite limit

Vertical Asymptotes

The line $x = a$ is a vertical asymptote if at least one of:

\[ \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \pm\infty, \quad \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = \pm\infty, \quad \text{or} \quad \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = \pm\infty \]
Vertical asymptote criteria

Finding VAs: For rational $f(x) = p(x)/q(x)$, check where $q(x) = 0$ and $p(x) \neq 0$. If both $p$ and $q$ are zero, you may have a hole instead.

Algebraic Forms Leading to Infinite Limits

FormBehavior
$\frac{k}{x - a}$ as $x \to a$$\pm\infty$ depending on sign of $k$ and side
$\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}$, $q(a)=0$, $p(a)\neq0$Vertical asymptote at $x = a$
$\ln|x|$ as $x \to 0^+$$-\infty$
$\ln|x|$ as $x \to 0^-$Undefined (approaches $-\infty$ from right)

Always check both sides: $\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = \infty$ and $\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = -\infty$ are both infinite, but they are not "equal." The two-sided limit DNE.

Limits at Infinity

Limits at infinity describe end behavior: what happens to $f(x)$ as $x \to \infty$ or $x \to -\infty$.

\[ \lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = L \iff \forall \epsilon > 0, \exists M > 0 : x > M \implies |f(x) - L| < \epsilon \]
Precise definition of limit at infinity

Horizontal and Oblique Asymptotes

For rational functions $f(x) = \dfrac{p(x)}{q(x)}$, compare degrees:

Degree of $p$ vs $q$Horizontal Asymptote
$\deg p < \deg q$$y = 0$
$\deg p = \deg q$$y = \text{ratio of leading coefficients}$
$\deg p = \deg q + 1$Oblique (slant) asymptote — use long division
$\deg p > \deg q + 1$No horizontal or oblique asymptote
\[ f(x) = \frac{3x^2 + 4x - 5}{9x^2 + 3x - 1} \implies \lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = \frac{3}{9} = \frac{1}{3} \]
Horizontal asymptote example (balanced)
\[ f(x) = \frac{3x^3 + 4x^2 + 2x + 1}{3x - 9} \implies \lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = \infty \text{ (no HA, degree diff > 1)} \]
No horizontal asymptote (top-heavy)

Horizontal Asymptote Rules for Rational Functions

  1. Bottom Heavy: If $\deg q > \deg p$, HA is $y = 0$.
  2. Balanced: If $\deg p = \deg q$, HA is ratio of leading coefficients.
  3. Top Heavy (degree diff = 1): Perform polynomial long division to find the slant asymptote.
  4. Top Heavy (degree diff > 1): No horizontal or oblique asymptote.

Remember: Horizontal asymptotes describe end behavior. A function can cross its HA any number of times.

Continuity on Intervals

A function is continuous on an interval if it is continuous at every point in that interval.

Interval TypeCondition
Open interval $(a, b)$Continuous at every point in $(a, b)$
Closed interval $[a, b]$Continuous on $(a, b)$ AND $\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = f(a)$ AND $\lim_{x \to b^-} f(x) = f(b)$

Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT)

\[ \text{If } f \text{ is continuous on } [a, b] \text{ and } N \text{ is between } f(a) \text{ and } f(b), \text{ then } \exists c \in [a, b] : f(c) = N. \]
Intermediate Value Theorem

IVT guarantees existence, not uniqueness: There may be multiple values of $c$ satisfying $f(c) = N$.

Root-finding application: If $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ have opposite signs and $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, then $f(c) = 0$ for some $c \in (a, b)$.

Continuity on Closed Intervals

A function continuous on a closed interval $[a, b]$ satisfies:

  • Continuous at every interior point of $(a, b)$
  • $\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = f(a)$ (right-continuous at $a$)
  • $\lim_{x \to b^-} f(x) = f(b)$ (left-continuous at $b$)

Common mistake: For the closed interval, you must check one-sided continuity at the endpoints, not just interior points.

Practice Problems

  1. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 5} \dfrac{x^2 + 2x - 35}{x^2 - 25}$ by factoring.
  2. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to -\infty} \dfrac{3x^2 + 5x - 2}{-2x + 4}$ (compare leading terms).
  3. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 3} \dfrac{x^2 - 9}{2 - \sqrt{x+1}}$ by rationalizing the denominator.
  4. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\frac{1}{x-4} + \frac{1}{4}}{x}$ by finding the LCD.
  5. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 1} \left(\frac{1}{x-1} - \frac{2}{x^2-1}\right)$ by combining fractions.
  6. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 5} \dfrac{\sqrt{x+4} - 2}{3 - \sqrt{x-1}}$ by rationalizing.
  7. Find all vertical and horizontal asymptotes of $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 3}{2x - 4}$.
  8. Use IVT to show $f(x) = x^3 - x - 1$ has a root in $[1, 2]$.
  9. Classify the discontinuities of $f(x) = \dfrac{|x-1|}{x^2 - 1}$.
  10. Find the slant asymptote of $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 3}{2x - 4}$ using long division.
